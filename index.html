Mark up

By the time you look, the arrow is in your hand.

The canvas opens the moment the capture lands, with the arrow tool already selected. No save dialog, no "choose a mode" in between.

The arrow is born thin where you start dragging and swells toward the point it's making — one filled shape, not a line wearing a hat. Pink, obviously.

Nine colors and a picker for any other — the last eight you chose stay one click away.

Every annotation is an object: reselect, move, delete at will, with practically unlimited undo. Draw without fear.

Hand off

Grab the tab, drop it into Slack.

When you're done drawing, just drag the tab at the bottom edge. Slack, Mail, Finder, your terminal — it lands as a real image file.

Zero dialogs along the way. The whole export–save–attach ritual simply disappears.

Prefer the clipboard? ⌘⇧C copies and closes in one shot.

Shoot

Same region, again.

It remembers your last selection and re-shoots it in one move. On days you capture the same screen over and over — docs, bug reports — nothing saves more time.

Window capture is one key, too. Stop tracing pixel edges by hand.

Record

When it moves, show it moving.

Screen recording, three ways: full screen, area, or window. Click highlights toggle with one tap.

Export is a footer switch between MP4 and GIF. Quality is one dropdown: High, Medium, Low — pick "Medium" and forget it; drop to "Low" when you need a light file for Slack. No forest of dials.

Mark up video

Draw on the recording, too.

The same arrows, shapes, pen, stamps, numbers, and text you use on stills — now on your recordings. Draw them straight onto the video; on export, everything bakes into the file.

Trim used to mean cropping the ends. Now it cuts the middle, too — pick a stretch you don't want and Cut it out. The awkward pause, the wrong window, the step you fumbled and redid: gone in one cut, without re-recording.

Each annotation carries its own start and end time, so an arrow can appear only when it's needed. And a swipe of Pixelate covers names, addresses, or notifications caught in the frame — before the file ever leaves your Mac.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

When can I use it?

Right now. FletchShot is on the Mac App Store — free to download, free to try for 14 days, then a one-time unlock. No account. If you need a heads-up when the next major version lands, there's an optional email box at the bottom of this FAQ.

Is there a free trial?

Yes — 14 days, everything on. Download the app for free, tap "Start 14-day trial" once inside the app, and saving, copying, and sharing work exactly like the paid version until the trial ends. Nothing is charged during the trial (Apple takes payment only when you tap "Unlock" at $9.99). After the 14 days, capturing and drawing stay free forever; the export routes — file save, clipboard copy, drag-away tab, share sheet — are what ask you to unlock. It's a one-time purchase, so there's nothing to auto-renew.

How much will it cost?

Free to download, free for a 14-day trial. After that, a one-time $9.99 unlock keeps saving, copying, and sharing available forever. No subscription. Family Sharing is off, so the unlock is per Apple ID. Major upgrades may someday be paid, but the version you buy keeps working.

Can I switch from Skitch?

That's exactly what it's built for. The tapered arrow's feel, the shot-to-annotation immediacy, and the drag-away tab were measured against a real copy of Skitch 2.9 and rebuilt to match. The default hotkey is ⌥⌘4, too. "A Skitch hand can use it without relearning anything" is our most important acceptance test.

Why zero network calls?

Because screenshots are full of secrets. FletchShot's spec pins networking at zero: OCR runs on-device, and neither screenshots nor recordings ever leave your Mac.

How is this different from CleanShot X?

CleanShot X is a genuinely excellent pro tool with 50+ features and deep settings. FletchShot goes the other way: it's built around doing "shoot → arrow → hand off" thirty times a day, three seconds each. We carry the best-loved features in light versions, but we don't compete on settings depth — and we deliberately have no cloud and no accounts.

What does it run on?

macOS 14 or later, native on Apple Silicon. Distributed through the Mac App Store (App Sandbox).
